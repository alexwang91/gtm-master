# Stop-Slop Editorial Gate

This gate adapts the public `hardikpandya/stop-slop` skill for GTM dashboards.
Source: https://github.com/hardikpandya/stop-slop. License: MIT.

Use this as an editorial filter, not as a license to make unsupported claims.

## Gate Sequence

1. Cut throat-clearing.
2. Replace vague claims with specific facts, numbers, owners, or actions.
3. Remove formulaic contrasts. State the recommendation directly.
4. Prefer active voice with a clear actor.
5. Vary sentence length.
6. Delete slogan-like endings.
7. Replace generic consulting words with the actual business mechanism.
8. Score the final copy.

## Banned Patterns

Avoid these patterns in dashboard-facing prose:

```text
In today's landscape
It is important to note
This is not just X, it is Y
unlock potential
game-changing
seamless
robust
leverage synergies
delve into
strategic imperative
holistic approach
key takeaway: [generic summary]
```

## Rewrite Rules

Weak:

```text
The product has a strong opportunity to win because the market is ready.
```

Better:

```text
Start with mid-market teams that already pay for manual reporting. They have a budget owner, a visible pain, and a short path to proof.
```

Weak:

```text
The channel strategy should leverage multiple touchpoints.
```

Better:

```text
Use LinkedIn ads to find the buyer, partner webinars to build trust, and sales calls to convert teams above 50 seats.
```

## Scoring

Score 1 to 10 for each dimension:

| Dimension | Test |
|---|---|
| Directness | Does the sentence make a claim without announcing itself? |
| Specificity | Does it name the customer, action, number, channel, or owner? |
| Rhythm | Do sentence lengths vary? |
| Trust | Does it avoid explaining obvious points? |
| Density | Can any phrase be cut without losing meaning? |

Revise when total score is below 40 out of 50.

## Dashboard Copy Checklist

- The executive answer can be read in 30 seconds.
- Every recommendation has a named owner or owner type.
- Every big claim has evidence, confidence, or a visible data gap.
- No section ends with a generic inspirational line.
- No paragraph uses the same rhythm three times in a row.
- No unsupported superlatives.

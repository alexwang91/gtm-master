# 17-Section Hardware GTM Readiness Rubric

Use this rubric when S00 needs a current-state view before planning, when S08
needs forecast confidence caps, or when S13 must explain which validation tests
matter first. Score each section 0-5 from supplied files, public evidence, or
explicit user hypotheses. Mark unavailable sections as gaps rather than inventing
readiness.

Total = sum of all scored sections. The total is less important than the shape:
where readiness clusters, where evidence is thin, and which gap blocks the next
commercial decision.

| # | Section | What Is Scored |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Positioning | Category claim, target shelf, differentiation, and consistency across product, retail, and media surfaces. |
| 2 | Localized consumer voice | Target-country reviews, forum language, YouTube comments, search terms, pain/praise/trigger coverage, and source bias labeling. |
| 3 | Product proof | Feature/spec proof, demo evidence, certification, durability, battery, compatibility, privacy, or safety support. |
| 4 | Competitor and substitute map | Direct competitors, previous generation, premium/budget anchors, substitutes, and jump-band rivals. |
| 5 | Price confidence | Local price corridor, WTP hypotheses, channel margin constraints, promo policy, and private COGS gap handling. |
| 6 | Channel readiness | Named local e-commerce, retail, operator, marketplace, own-store, and service/support channels with capability notes. |
| 7 | Retail/PDP readiness | PDP copy, proof assets, comparison table, FAQ, trust policy, warranty, returns, availability, and mobile readability. |
| 8 | Message architecture | Segment message fit, feature-benefit-proof chain, objection handling, claim risk, and local language seeds. |
| 9 | Creator/media readiness | Creator archetypes, candidate evidence, expected outcome range, budget basis, brand-safety, and disclosure risk. |
| 10 | Launch plan readiness | ORB channel mix, launch phase calendar, owned/rented/borrowed coordination, and launch asset preparedness. |
| 11 | Forecast readiness | TAM/SAM/SOM seed, segment split, channel split, lifecycle curve, marketing response, inventory, and confidence caps. |
| 12 | Measurement readiness | Analytics events, UTM, channel tracking, sell-in/sell-through separation, retailer reporting, and post-launch learning loop. |
| 13 | Validation readiness | Data gap inventory, ICE-prioritized experiment backlog, pass/fail rules, sample access, and decision deadlines. |
| 14 | Post-purchase activation | Setup, pairing, sizing, onboarding, education, warranty, support, and return-prevention readiness. |
| 15 | Review and advocacy loop | NSS/NPS proxy, earned growth seed, review generation, referral/recommendation triggers, and quote usage policy. |
| 16 | Internationalization | Local language, currency, retailer norms, payment/finance options, compliance uncertainty, and local proof requirements. |
| 17 | Operating model | Owners, budget, agency/contractor fit, approval workflow, and state-machine readiness for long execution. |

## Score Guide

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | No material, no credible hypothesis, or not applicable without explanation. |
| 1 | Weak hypothesis only; no target-country support. |
| 2 | Some evidence or user input, but stale, narrow, or not decision-grade. |
| 3 | Usable directional evidence with visible caveats and gaps. |
| 4 | Strong cross-source evidence or private data, plus clear action implications. |
| 5 | Decision-grade evidence, owned metric history, and repeatable process. |

## Shape Interpretation

After scoring, write a 2-4 sentence shape interpretation:

```text
High in {strong sections}, low in {weak sections}. The next decision is blocked
most by {gap}. S13 should prioritize {validation theme}, while S14 should show
the gap visibly rather than smoothing it over.
```

## Hardware Notes

- A low paid-media score is not a weakness when the product is pre-launch and has no ad account access.
- A low post-purchase score is a serious risk for setup-heavy, health-adjacent, app-paired, or high-return categories.
- A high price score requires both public price anchors and private or explicit-gap margin handling.
- A high channel score requires named local channels, not generic labels like "retail" or "e-commerce".
